## Q1. What is a Venn Diagram in Probability?
A **Venn diagram** is a graphical representation used in probability to show **relationships between different events**. Events are represented as **circles**, and the overlapping areas illustrate how events are related.

📍 It helps visualize:
- Common outcomes
- Differences between events
- Probability calculations

---

## Q2. How Are Venn Diagrams Used to Represent Events and Their Relationships?
Venn diagrams use **circles inside a universal set** to represent events.

They show:
- Overlapping areas → shared outcomes
- Separate areas → independent outcomes
- Entire circles → total outcomes of an event

This visual approach simplifies complex probability relationships.

---

## Q3 & Q4. Union, Intersection, and Complement of Events

### 🔹 Union (A ∪ B)
Represents outcomes that belong to **Event A OR Event B or both**.

### 🔹 Intersection (A ∩ B)
Represents outcomes common to **both Event A AND Event B**.

### 🔹 Complement (A′)
Represents outcomes **not belonging** to Event A.

---

## Q4. Example: Mutually Exclusive Events
Two events are **mutually exclusive** if they **cannot occur at the same time**.

📌 Example:
- Event A: Rolling a 2 on a die
- Event B: Rolling a 5 on a die

Since both cannot occur together:
- A ∩ B = ∅

Venn diagram shows **no overlap** between the circles.

---

## Q5. What Is a Contingency Table?
A **contingency table** (or cross-tabulation) is a table used to display the **frequency distribution of two or more categorical variables**.

It helps identify relationships between variables.

---

## Q6. How Is a Contingency Table Used?
A contingency table organizes data into:
- Rows → One categorical variable
- Columns → Another categorical variable

📍 Benefits:
- Easy comparison
- Pattern recognition
- Probability calculation

---

## Q7. What Is Marginal Probability?
**Marginal probability** is the probability of a **single event occurring**, regardless of other variables.

📐 Calculation:
\[ P(A) = \frac{Total\ outcomes\ of\ A}{Total\ observations} \]

It is calculated using **row or column totals** of a contingency table.

---

## Q8. Real-World Meaning of Marginal Probability
Marginal probability represents the **overall likelihood** of an event.

📌 Example:
- Probability that a randomly selected customer is male
- Probability that a student passes an exam

It ignores other conditions and focuses on **one variable only**.

---

## Q9. What Is Joint Probability?
**Joint probability** is the probability of **two or more events occurring together**.

📐 Notation:
\[ P(A \cap B) \]

It focuses on **combined outcomes**.

---

## Q10. Difference Between Joint and Marginal Probability

| Aspect | Marginal Probability | Joint Probability |
|------|---------------------|------------------|
| Focus | One event | Two or more events |
| Example | P(A) | P(A ∩ B) |
| Source | Row/column total | Cell value |

---

## Q11. Example: Joint Probability from Contingency Table

📊 Suppose a table shows:
- Students who are **Male and Passed** = 30
- Total students = 100

📐 Joint Probability:
\[ P(Male ∩ Passed) = \frac{30}{100} = 0.30 \]
