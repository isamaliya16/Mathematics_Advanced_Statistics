# 📊 Inferential Statistics – 
---

## Q.1 Define Inferential Statistics in your own words with one real-life example

**Inferential Statistics** is the branch of statistics that uses data from a sample to make predictions, estimates, or decisions about a larger population.

**Example:** A teacher checks the marks of 30 students to estimate the average performance of all students in the college.

---

## Q.2 List the major differences between Descriptive and Inferential Statistics

| Descriptive Statistics | Inferential Statistics |
|-----------------------|------------------------|
| Summarizes data | Makes predictions or decisions |
| Uses charts, mean, median | Uses probability and tests |
| No conclusion beyond data | Draws conclusions about population |
| Example: Average marks | Example: Predict future results |

---

## Q.3 Explain the importance of sample and population in Inferential Statistics

- **Population** is the complete group we want to study.
- **Sample** is a small part of the population used for analysis.

Inferential statistics uses samples because studying the entire population is often costly or impossible.

---

## Q.4 Describe one situation where collecting data from the entire population is not possible

In election surveys, it is impossible to ask every voter in the country, so a sample of voters is surveyed to predict election results.

---

## Q.5 What is a confidence interval? (2–3 lines)

A **confidence interval** is a range of values within which the true population parameter is expected to lie with a certain level of confidence (such as 95%).

---

## Q.6 What is meant by hypothesis testing? Why is it used in statistics?

**Hypothesis testing** is a statistical method used to decide whether a claim about a population is true or false based on sample data.

It is used to make objective decisions and validate assumptions.

---

## Q.7 Write down any 3 practical examples where hypothesis testing can be used

1. Testing whether a new medicine is more effective than the old one.
2. Checking if a factory’s average product weight meets standards.
3. Verifying if online learning improves student performance.

---

## Q.8 Describe the basic idea behind "testing a claim" using statistics

Testing a claim involves collecting sample data and using statistical methods to decide whether the claim is supported or rejected.

---

## Q.9 What is meant by the term significance level (α)? Write its usual values

The **significance level (α)** is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is actually true.

**Common values:** 0.05, 0.01, 0.10

---

## Q.10 What is the meaning of p-value in simple terms?

The **p-value** tells us how likely the observed result is if the null hypothesis is true.

A smaller p-value indicates stronger evidence against the null hypothesis.

---

## Q.11 Define the Null Hypothesis (H₀) and Alternate Hypothesis (H₁)

- **Null Hypothesis (H₀):** A statement that there is no effect or no difference.
- **Alternate Hypothesis (H₁):** A statement that contradicts the null hypothesis.

---

## Q.12 Write Null and Alternate Hypotheses

**Claim:** A company claims their average delivery time is 2 days.

- **H₀:** Average delivery time = 2 days
- **H₁:** Average delivery time ≠ 2 days

---

## Q.13 Explain what it means to "reject the null hypothesis"

Rejecting the null hypothesis means the sample data provides enough evidence to support the alternate hypothesis.

---

## Q.14 Give an example of a research question with H₀ and H₁

**Research Question:** Does a new teaching method improve exam scores?

- **H₀:** The new teaching method does not improve exam scores.
- **H₁:** The new teaching method improves exam scores.

---

## Q.15 Why do we need both null and alternate hypotheses?

Both hypotheses are needed to make a clear decision framework and ensure objective statistical testing.
